DISSERTATION ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS

Purpose
This document brings together the assumptions, placeholders and limitations
identified in Objectives 1, 2 and 3. It should be read alongside the results
tables. The points below do not invalidate the analysis; they define the
scope in which its findings should be interpreted.

Objective 1: Next-day PV forecasting

1. The final comparison uses one genuine unseen test week: 1-7 March 2025.
   The reported daytime evaluation window is 06:00-18:59 and contains 91
   hourly observations. Results from a single week may not represent all
   seasons, weather conditions or plant operating conditions.

2. January 2024-January 2025 production data use verified plant-level
   aggregate production fields. February and March 2025 exports did not
   contain a certified plant-level aggregate. Their production values were
   derived by summing the inverter values that were available at each time.
   These rows are labelled derived_partial_sum and are not equivalent in
   certainty to verified full-plant readings.

3. The derived February/March production data had about 77% average daytime
   inverter coverage. Inv24 and Inv25 were absent in February; Inv18 and
   Inv19 stopped reporting during February; and all four were absent from the
   March exports. The March test results therefore describe the best available
   partial-coverage data rather than certified total plant generation.

4. Installed capacity by inverter was not available. It was therefore not
   possible to apply a true capacity-weighted completeness threshold or to
   determine whether the missing channels represented offline equipment,
   changed reporting, or another data issue. A corrected export or maintenance
   confirmation would strengthen future analysis.

5. XGBoost was the best standalone model on this test week. The two hybrid
   models used XGBoost and SARIMAX, with residual correctors trained from
   out-of-fold validation residuals only. Both hybrids underperformed the
   XGBoost standalone model on the final test week. Hybridisation should not
   therefore be presented as an automatic improvement.

6. Prophet followed the general daylight pattern but underpredicted the March
   peak and produced negative predictions at dawn and evening. Its weaker
   performance is a result for this dataset and test period, rather than a
   general statement that Prophet is unsuitable for all solar PV forecasts.

Objective 2: Forecast uncertainty

1. Forecast error is defined as actual generation minus forecast generation.
   Positive error means the model under-forecast; negative error means the
   model over-forecast.

2. The primary uncertainty estimates use the same 91 daytime observations
   from the March Week 1 test period. The supplementary rolling backtest has
   819 daytime observations across nine seven-day origins, but it is a
   robustness check and does not replace the genuine March test-week result.

3. The 12:00-13:00 error distribution used for the commitment analysis has
   only 7 observations, one for each day in the test week. Its percentiles and
   quantiles are descriptive and have substantial sampling uncertainty; they
   should not be treated as stable population estimates.

4. The March primary bias, the larger rolling-backtest bias and the 12:00-13:00
   bias differ in direction. This is insufficient evidence to claim a seasonal
   effect. The difference may reflect sampling variation, period-specific
   conditions or partial inverter coverage.

5. Relative uncertainty was calculated against the absolute forecast value.
   Capacity-relative uncertainty was not calculated because an authoritative
   plant rated capacity in kW or kWp was not available. This should be added
   only after the true rated capacity is confirmed.

6. March forecast errors are measured against derived partial-coverage actual
   generation, not certified full-plant output. The approximately 77% daytime
   inverter coverage is therefore also a limitation on the uncertainty
   estimates.

Objective 3: Market energy commitment

1. Commitments are scenario-based, not historical market-settlement results.
   The cost assumptions are: Conservative, Cu = KSh 5/kWh and Co = KSh
   15/kWh, giving q* = 0.25; Balanced, Cu = KSh 10/kWh and Co = KSh 10/kWh,
   giving q* = 0.50; and Aggressive, Cu = KSh 15/kWh and Co = KSh 5/kWh,
   giving q* = 0.75. In each scenario, Cu + Co = KSh 20/kWh. These values
   express different relative consequences of under- and over-commitment and
   must be replaced or justified with actual market rules where available.

2. The KSh 15/kWh energy price is an illustrative assumption, not a verified
   Strathmore tariff, feed-in rate or settlement price. As of 2026, Kenya
   Power base commercial/domestic rates plus fuel and forex adjustments
   typically put effective delivered electricity prices somewhat above KSh
   15/kWh. It is therefore a conservative round placeholder, not an
   upper-bound estimate. Confirmed Strathmore settlement or feed-in pricing
   should replace it when available.

3. Commitment is floored at zero and capped at 350,872 W (350.872 kWh for the
   one-hour interval). This is a provisional soft cap based on observed data,
   not the plant's true rated capacity. It must be replaced once the
   authoritative plant rating is available.

4. The headline calculation uses 3 March 2025 because it had the highest mean
   daytime inverter coverage in the test week, 78.30%. The full seven-day
   appendix is retained so that the headline day is not interpreted as the
   only possible operational result.

5. Actual generation was used only after the commitment decision to calculate
   shortfall, surplus and illustrative imbalance cost. It was not used to set
   the commitment.

6. The commitment quantiles come from the 7-observation midday error sample.
   Together with the partial March inverter coverage, this means that the
   economic outcomes are provisional scenario calculations rather than
   bankable market-settlement estimates.

7. The commitment results use the same partial-coverage March production data
   as Objectives 1 and 2. They should be updated if certified full-plant
   February/March production data, rated capacity, tariff information or
   market imbalance rules become available.
